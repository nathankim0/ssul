# Conflicts between source skills, and how ssul resolves them

ssul was built by running many open-source writing skills together on real Korean Threads posts. They disagree in several places. These are the rulings ssul follows.

| # | Conflict | Ruling |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Rhythm.** One Threads skill says uniform rhythm wins and never to chop sentences. Storytelling skills say vary sentence length. Korean editing skills say never force variety. Spoken-register checkers ban the same ending 4 times in a row. | Do not manufacture variety. Endings follow the voice source; see 29. Line breaks follow breath, not a pattern. |
| 2 | **Contrast hooks vs the "not X but Y" ban.** Hook libraries use A-vs-B contrast. Anti-AI skills call "X가 아니라 Y" the top AI tell. | Allowed only when Y is concrete (a number, a scene, a quote) and the post delivers it. Never as the closing line. Vague Y (본질, 마인드셋, 방향성) is always cut. |
| 3 | **"You" framing vs first-person stories.** Hook audits flag "I" openers as irrelevant to the reader. Story skills say personal stories are first-person by nature. | First person is fine when the moment is one the reader has lived. Do not force "여러분" or "당신". |
| 4 | **Specificity vs no invention.** Every source says add numbers and names. Every source also says never invent them. | Specifics come only from the user or the conversation. Otherwise write them in plainly so the post is paste-ready, and list each invented one in the notes. A plausible invented number is worse than none. |
| 5 | **Self-questions and CTAs vs engagement bait.** Spoken-register guides recommend a self-question every few paragraphs. Anti-AI skills ban "어떻게 생각하세요? 👇". | At most one question per post. It must be easy to answer (a number, yes or no, the reader's own case). No pointing emoji, no "댓글로 알려주세요" without a reason. |
| 6 | **Lists vs no bullets.** Korean editing skills ban bullets and bold in prose. Threads formulas use numbered lists. | Use a list only when the content is a list. Plain `1.` lines, no bold, no dashes, no nested items. A daily post is never a list. |
| 7 | **Register.** Some Korean skills treat `~함` as normal (it is, in documents). Spoken-register skills want 해요체. Real AI and dev Threads feeds are mostly 반말 혼잣말. | Follow, in order: the user's own posts, pasted samples, then the persona default (`personas.md`). `sns` keeps one register per post; `farmer` and `midlife` may mix 해요체 and 합니다 as real posts do. |
| 8 | **Slang vs "do not add what is not there".** Slang skills add trending words for wit. Editing skills forbid adding jokes or tone the author did not write. | Slang only in draft mode and only when the user's voice already uses it. Never inject slang while polishing someone else's text. Slang older than a few months reads as trying too hard. |
| 9 | **English formulas vs 번역투.** The best hook libraries are English. Translating them word for word produces 번역투. | Borrow the mechanism, rewrite the words. Banned calques: "~하는 사람 99%가 모르는", "게임체인저", "당신은 ~하고 있나요?", "이것은 ~입니다". |
| 10 | **Structure vs weight.** Viral frameworks add setup, lists, and morals. Users read the result as heavy. | Casual posts skip setup and morals. If the post would work without a line, cut the line. |
| 11 | **Context.** Drafts built from a long conversation lean on terms only that conversation understands. | Stranger test on every post: zero-context readers must follow every line. |
| 12 | **Auto-publishing.** Some Threads and X skills publish through APIs. | ssul never publishes. It returns text. Posting is the user's decision. |
| 13 | **Hashtags.** Sources range from 0 to 5. | 0 or 1, at the end. Threads allows one topic tag. |
| 14 | **Long reports vs the deliverable.** Audit skills print scores and tables. | Return the post first and at most 3 short notes. Full audits only in audit mode. |
| 15 | **Punchlines vs truth.** Story skills want a quotable last line. With no funny event, the result is a forced joke. | Do not manufacture humor. Without a real funny moment, switch to Finding, Hot take, or One-liner. Attitude beats a fake punchline. |
| 16 | **Using the session as material vs the stranger test.** The conversation is a source of real facts, but stories about the writing session itself are insider material. | Use the session's facts when they help a stranger (a finding, a tool warning). Do not write posts about the act of writing the post. |
| 17 | **Hook first vs disclosure first.** Hook skills want the strongest line on top. Korean disclosure rules require sponsorship to be stated in the title or first part. | Disclosure wins. A short separate first line (`[광고] OO 협찬`), then the hook. Never move disclosure to the end or a comment. |
| 18 | **Proofreading vs casual voice.** Proofreading skills fix every nonstandard spelling. Social posts use ㅋㅋ, contractions, and fragments on purpose. | Fix clear errors (되/돼, 안/않, 할께요). Leave intentional casual style. When unsure, keep it and mention it. |
| 19 | **Editing for voice vs preserving meaning.** Humanizers shorten and rephrase. Meaning-first editors forbid touching negation, conditions, and limits. | Meaning wins in polish mode. Lock negation, conditions, numbers, and claims first; change only how it reads. |
| 20 | **Remembering the user vs privacy.** Voice and story-bank skills store the user's writing and stories. | Save to `~/.ssul/` only after the user agrees once, show what is saved, never store other people's private details. |
| 21 | **Posting-time rules vs evidence.** Some sources give best posting windows. No verified study covers Korea. | Do not cite a posting time. Suggest the user test when their own audience is online. |
| 22 | **Replies: engagement vs spam.** Reply skills push frequent replying. Anti-bait rules ban empty replies. | Every reply adds one specific thing (a fact, the user's case, a question, a reasoned counterpoint) and refers to the post itself. |
| 23 | **Emoji and symbols.** Several sources allow up to 2 emoji and use middle dots, circled numbers, and arrows for scannability. Readers now read these as AI output. | No emoji unless the voice source's real posts use them (some older communities do; match their rate). No `·`, no ①②③, no →. Use commas or 와/랑, plain `1.` numbering, and `->` or words. |
| 24 | **Crafted structure vs human texture.** Hook and story skills build a tight hook, contrast, and punchline. Real Korean feeds read rambling, unfinished, and direct. A post that is too neat reads as AI. | Hooks and punchlines are tools, not requirements. Scene, Confession, and Question posts may tell events in order and end on a real ask or just stop. |
| 25 | **One-question limit vs help posts.** Anti-bait rules allow one question. Help and opinion posts ("도와줘", "다들 어떻게 해?") are built on asking. | In Question posts, ask what the user really wants to know, up to two questions, addressed to followers. The bait ban still applies to fake questions. |
| 26 | **Consistent voice vs variation.** Voice skills match one fixed voice. Real accounts vary post to post, and a model's own "random" choice drifts to the same pattern. | Sources set the range; `scripts/roll.py` picks within it with a real random generator. The user's asks and voice profile narrow the range; serious tone removes ㅋㅋ. |
| 27 | **Every pass cuts vs real roughness.** Anti-AI and proofreading passes remove report words, 번역투, and errors, so each pass makes the post cleaner. For readers whose real posts are loose (many farmers and older writers), a clean post is itself the AI tell. | The voice source is the measure in both directions. A Too clean pass checks precision, connected beats, added turns, evenly spread endings, and spacing against the samples, and roughens to match. Never invent typos or feelings. |
| 28 | **Story formula vs a plain log.** Hook and story skills want a hook, beats joined by 근데 and 그래서, and a last line worth quoting. Farmers and older writers usually tell the day in order and stop. | Hook formulas and the turn belong to the `sns` persona and to `flow: turn`. `farmer`, `midlife`, and `flow: log` start and end the way the samples do, with no added turn. |
| 29 | **Ending variety vs ending repetition.** A spoken-register rule bans 4 identical endings in a row. Real posts repeat endings, and a rule against it produces endings spread so evenly they read as generated. | Endings follow the voice source and the roll's `endings` setting. Repetition is allowed. Evenly spread endings are flagged. Only `sns` with `endings: mixed` treats 4 or more in a row as a drone. |
| 30 | **Samples for topics vs samples for voice.** Some prompts paste popular posts and say to use them for topics only. Those posts are the best voice evidence available. | Pasted real posts are a voice reference by default (measured into a voice card); their text, numbers, and stories are never reused. Skip only when the user says not to follow their voice. |
| 31 | **The 2030 default vs other readers.** The original rules, examples, and endings were built on 2030 Threads feeds. | The persona sets the defaults. `sns` keeps every earlier rule; `farmer`, `midlife`, and `custom` change register, flow, hooks, laugh marks, and emoji to match their own readers. |
| 32 | **Banned openers and aphorisms vs farming boards.** Hook skills ban greetings and self-introductions as first lines; anti-AI skills ban aphorism endings. On farming boards, 안녕하세요, where and what the writer farms, a hedged saying, and a well-wish are the most common openers and closers. | Per persona. `sns` keeps the bans. `farmer` and `midlife` may open with a greeting or an introduction and close with a hedged saying or a wish when the samples do it. Invented sayings with no material behind them are still cut. |
| 33 | **Rough voice vs precise facts.** The Too clean pass treats sharp numbers as a tell. Real farmers name the product, the mix ratio, the distance, and the temperature range exactly. | Precision the material has is kept and stated the way farmers say it (약, 정도, 여, `~`). Precision the material lacks is the tell. Vague farming talk where a real post would name the product is also a tell. |
| 34 | **No fake typos vs real rough text.** ssul never injected typos, so every farm post came out with perfect spacing. Real posts on farming boards have loose spacing and habitual spellings, and the model will not produce them on its own. | `roughen.py` applies loose spacing and 1 to 3 habitual spellings by roll for `farm` and `plain` only, never in numbers, names, or negation. Random typos are still banned. Shape and topic are rolled too, because the model's own choice converges on one post. |
