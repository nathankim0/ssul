# Shapes

Examples below show a shape only. Never reuse their topics, wording, numbers, or line order. The `[brackets]` in them mark details that were not real; output never contains brackets.

Pick by what the material has: an event with a turn (Scene, Confession), usable facts (Finding, List), an attitude (Hot take, One-liner), or a day told in order (Log). Do not add a turn the material does not have.

## Scene (장면) (likes)
First line drops the reader into the moment. Two to four beats joined by 근데 or 그래서. Ends on a quote, an image, or a small punchline.

> 기상청 앱에 내일 아침 [영하 2도] 뜨자마자
> [아빠]가 밥 먹다 말고 숟가락 딱 내려놓더니 "무 뽑자" 이러시고
>
> 헤드랜턴 켜고 나갔죠
> 무청은 내일 자르자 했는데 결국 [열 시]까지 다 잘랐거든요
>
> 근데 오늘 아침 보니까 [1도]
> 서리요? 하나도 안 왔어요
>
> [아빠]는 "[그래도 뽑아놓길 잘했지]" 하시는데
> 내년에도 똑같이 하실 거 알잖아요 ㅋㅋ

## Confession (고백) (replies)
One true thing, including the unflattering part. The reader replies because they did it too.

> 클로드 설정 파일에 이렇게 적어놨어요
>
> "묻지 말고 끝까지 해.
> 애매하면 알아서 고르고, 뭘 골랐는지만 나중에 알려줘.
> 대신 뭘 지우거나, 배포하거나, 밖으로 메시지 보낼 때는 꼭 먼저 물어봐."
>
> 적고 나서 다시 읽어보니까
> 회사에서 신입한테 해주는 말이랑 똑같네요 ㅋㅋ

## Reversal (뒤집기) (reposts)
A common belief, then what actually happened. Both concrete. The body proves the second half.

## List (목록) (saves)
Only when the content is a list. One line per item, plain `1.`, no bold. Under 6 items. Intro is one line. No moral at the end; one easy question at most.

## Question (질문) (replies)
One real question the user wants answered, with enough context in one or two lines for strangers to answer it.

## One-liner (한두 줄) (likes, reposts)
One feeling or one verdict. No setup, no explanation. If a second line exists, it twists the first.

> AI 티 안 나게 글 써주는 스킬 16개 깔아봄
> 결론: AI 티는 안 나는데 재미도 안 남

## Finding (발견) (saves, reposts)
The user tried something and learned facts others can use. Title line says what was tried; numbered facts; one closing line on what the user does now. Every item must be checked, not remembered.

> 클로드 스킬 GitHub에서 16개 깔아보면서 알게 된 거
>
> 1. 어떤 플러그인은 깔면 내가 메시지 보낼 때마다 스크립트가 돎
> 2. 어떤 스킬은 지침이 파일에 없고 쓸 때마다 인터넷에서 받아옴. 내용이 언제든 바뀔 수 있다는 뜻
> 3. 라이선스 없는 레포도 있음. 이건 가져다 쓰면 안 됨
>
> 그래서 이제 깔기 전에 SKILL.md랑 hooks 폴더는 꼭 열어봄

## Hot take (한마디) (replies, quotes)
An attitude about a trend: 짜증, 냉소, 자조. Name the trend concretely, give the user's reaction, stop. Self-mockery lands better than mocking others; never target a named private person.

## Log (일지) (replies, likes)
The day in order: the weather or the date, what was done, how the crop, the animals, or the body is, then a worry, a wish, an ask, or a greeting. No hook, no turn, no punchline. The usual shape for `farmer` and `midlife` (`personas.md`), and fine for anyone with `flow: log`. Endings may repeat; asides stay in.

## Ask (질문 글) (farming boards; replies)
The most common farming-board post. Context a stranger needs first (region, crop, variety, tree age, what was already done, the forecast), then the question in plain 합니다체. May open with 안녕하세요 and where and what the writer farms; may address 전문가님 or 선배님. Product names and ratios exact, from the material, or invented and listed in the notes.

## Photo caption (사진 글) (farming boards)
The post is the photo; the text names it. One line of nouns naming the work in the photo, or the crop name plus 입니다 and one line of what the writer wants to know or show. Ask the user what the photo shows; never describe a photo you have not been told about.

## Essay (긴 글) (farming boards)
A long post that wanders: the work, a memory, family, how-to tips, the weather, a hedged saying, a wish for readers. Only when the user's material is that rich; never pad. Every memory and tip comes from the user.

## Notice (판매 글) (farming boards)
The user's own produce or seedlings for sale: what it is, one real detail (how orders are going, the harvest), and where to order. Own products need no 광고 label; anything received from a third party does (`disclosure.md`).

## Threads limits
- 500 characters per post. Split into a thread only when each post can stand alone.
- 0 or 1 hashtag. No link in the first post. No emoji (unless the voice source uses them), middle dots, circled numbers, or Unicode arrows.
