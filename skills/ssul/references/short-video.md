# Short video: script, on-screen text, caption

Use when the user wants a Reels, Shorts, or TikTok script, on-screen text, or a caption for a video. Everything else in ssul still applies: real material, register, AI tells, disclosure.

## The first 2 seconds

The hook has three layers, all landing at once:

1. **Visual**: the first frame already shows the subject or the result (the radish in the frost, the before and after). Never a logo or a greeting.
2. **Spoken line**: the first line from `hooks.md`, said in under 2 seconds.
3. **On-screen text**: 3 to 7 words, readable with sound off. Most people watch muted.

## Script

- Write the last line first, then beats joined by 근데 / 그래서 (same as posts).
- Spoken Korean, not written: the endings people actually say, short sentences.
- Read it aloud with a timer. Cut beats, not the hook or the last line, until it fits the target length.
- Format per line: `[visual] / spoken line / on-screen text`.
- Payoff before the end; do not hold the only useful part for the last second.

## On-screen text

- 1 line per beat, 3 to 9 words, high contrast, placed away from the platform's buttons at the bottom and right.
- Korean subtitles of everything said, or at least the key lines.
- No emoji, middle dots, circled numbers, or Unicode arrows in on-screen text or the caption.

## Caption

- First line repeats or extends the hook (it shows before "더보기").
- 1 to 3 short lines of context the video does not say.
- At most one call to action that fits: save it (for info), send it to someone (for relatable), comment with one easy answer (for a question). No "좋아요 구독 알림설정" chains, no "댓글에 OO 남기면 보내드림" bait.
- 0 to 3 relevant hashtags; they help a little with topic matching, not with reach.
- Sponsored? Disclosure in the video and in the caption's first line (`disclosure.md`).

## Platform notes

- **Reels / TikTok**: sends and saves are strong signals; aim the CTA at those.
- **Shorts**: can link to a related long video; mention it only if it exists.
- Use only music the account is licensed to use; suggest the platform's own library.
